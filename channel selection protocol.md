# Channel-selection protocol for tracker v4

*Items 10 and 11: building and choosing the claim gaps. Committed before anything is fitted*

November 20, 2026. Decisions A, B and C, and the transfer-out source, approved November 20. Field names confirmed by the field check (data/v4 channels/field check.json).

## 1. What this protocol fixes

RICD 17.7 (Spine §15.2.1) defines the claim gaps tracker v4 can use and requires that the ones entering v4 be chosen on development data, never on test outcomes, in five steps, each completed and committed before the next: (1) fitting the calibrations, (2) selecting the dimensions, (3) fixing the thresholds, (4) fixing the prediction models, and (5) scoring and evaluating held-out colleges. This protocol covers steps 1 and 2 for the three IPEDS gaps of item 10 (the price gap, the outcome gap and the four-year gap) and the comparison of item 11. The soundness gap (the meta channel) and the transfer gap are built separately; the transfer gap applies mainly to two-year colleges and is fixed with the two-year interim protocol.

Everything below is committed to the repository before any calibration is fitted. Field names below were confirmed in the data dictionaries by the field check (data/v4 channels/field check.json, workflow v4_field_check.yml), including the older .xls dictionaries of about 2009 to 2013; where a field changes between years, the change starts a new comparability segment, as the Spine requires, and a field missing from a year makes that dimension unavailable for that year rather than filled from another field.

## 2. Development data and the time split

The development colleges are four-year institutions scored at two scoring years, with outcomes from the IPEDS header files under the primary coding of protocol v2. Calibrations (step 1) use only input years; no closure outcome enters step 1. Selection (step 2) uses closure outcomes, split in time: stages 1 and 2 on the earlier scoring year, stage 3 (confirmation) on the later one.

- **Which colleges (Decision A, approved).** The current development set is 670 institutions with 70 closures: 425 scored at 2014 (59 closures, 46 of them for-profit, 13 private nonprofit, none public) and 245 scored at 2019 (11 closures). Stage 3 on the 2019 group would rest on 11 closures, about 6 of them nonprofit, too few to confirm anything. Decided: widen the development data to every four-year institution in each frame, scored for the gaps only (the gaps are computed directly from IPEDS and need no Bayesian scoring): the full 2014 frame (outcomes 2015–2019) for stages 1 and 2, and the full 2019 frame (outcomes 2019–2025, including the v3 test institutions) for stage 3. This is legitimate development use, because v4’s own tests are new outcomes (the two-year interim test and the HD2026 retest), and it raises the confirmation stage from 11 closures to the whole 2019 frame (about 1,980 institutions, with at least 80 closures among those the tracker itself could score, and more among the rest).
- **No look-ahead.** Every input for a scoring year t is taken only from IPEDS releases dated on or before t. Outcome Measures, first collected in 2015–16, is therefore unavailable for the 2014 scoring year, and completion there comes from the Graduation Rates files; the source of every completion figure is recorded and used as a stratification variable.
- **Calibration years.** Step 1 fits on college-years up to and including fiscal 2013 and is validated out of sample on fiscal 2014 to 2018, with its own diagnostics (calibration slope and mean absolute error by sector), then frozen. The retention calibration is fitted separately for each completion source, because the two sources define completion differently: for Graduation Rates completion on files through 2013, validated on 2014 to 2018; for Outcome Measures completion, which begins in 2015, on the 2015 and 2016 files, validated on 2017 and 2018. The calibration is a nondecreasing (isotonic) fit of the lower end of the completion band on retention, as Definition 15.2.10 requires.

## 3. The three gaps (as defined in Spine §15.2.1)

### 3.1 The price gap

- **Inputs.** Published price: full-time undergraduate tuition plus required fees from the Institutional Characteristics charges file (IC_AY: TUITION2 + FEE2 in-state, TUITION3 + FEE3 out-of-state), weighted by the share of first-time students residing in the institution’s own state (Fall Enrollment residence file EF C: EFRES01 counts by EFCSTATE, matched to the institution’s state in the header file; carried forward from the most recent collection year where the file is not collected). In-district rates (TUITION1 + FEE1) are not used, because in-district students cannot be counted in these files; public institutions are weighted at their in-state and out-of-state rates. Net tuition revenue: tuition and fees after discounts and allowances, by finance form (F1A: F1B01; F2: F2D01; F3: F3D01), over 12-month full-time-equivalent enrollment (EFIA: FTEUG + FTEGD + FTEDPP, reported values). Because net tuition includes graduate tuition while the published price is the undergraduate rate, the graduate share of FTE is recorded and is a stratification variable in every price-gap analysis.
- **Rules.** The realization ratio r_raw is reported unclipped; valid within 0.05 to 1.10; a valid ratio above 1 is kept and flagged in every output; outside that range the price dimension is unavailable for that year, with the reason recorded. Floor η_pr = 0.05.
- **Need-associated discount.** A fractional logit of the clipped discount rate on the shares of first-time, full-time federal-aid recipients in each IPEDS family-income band (SFA: GIS4N12, GIS4N22, GIS4N32, GIS4N42, GIS4N52 over GIS4N2), plus control; a separate fit on the Pell share (SFA: PGRNT_P) where the bands are missing; neither available, the price gap is unavailable. Fitted in step 1 and frozen. The excess-discount widening is recorded for every college and tested against prediction P1 only; it is not a selection feature.

### 3.2 The outcome gap

- **Reported completion and transfer-outs (decided November 20).** Outcome Measures publishes “enrolled subsequently at another institution” only at the eight-year status point, so both figures come from that point, for the same entering cohort: completion is the award rate at eight years (OM: OMAWDP8) and transfer-outs the share subsequently enrolled at another institution (OMENRAP), for the first-time, full-time cohort, from the newest Outcome Measures file released by the scoring year. Where no Outcome Measures file was yet released (the 2014 scoring year), both figures come from the same Graduation Rates file, so that they describe the same cohort: completion is the bachelor’s subcohort’s completers within 150% of normal time and transfer-outs its transfer-out students (GR: GRTYPE 16), each over the adjusted bachelor’s subcohort, with the GRTYPE codes read from each year’s dictionary labels at run time; where the college leaves transfer-outs blank they are unreported (Definition 15.2.9). Retention is taken for the same entering cohort as the completion figure (Fall Enrollment D of the year after entry). The source of every value is recorded and is a stratification variable.
- **Outside measures.** Retention of the same entering cohort (Fall Enrollment EF D: RET_PCF, the full-time retention rate), flagged as college-reported in every output; nursing licensure (NCLEX) in the pilot states only (Section 5). Each calibration h_e is fitted in step 1 against the lower end of the reported completion band and frozen.
- **Directions.** Overclaiming and underclaiming are recorded separately; the availability of τ_out is a stratification variable in every underclaiming analysis.

### 3.3 The four-year gap

- **Inputs.** Completion within 100% and 150% of normal time for the bachelor’s-seeking cohort (GR200: BANC100 and BANC150), on-time share r_on = BANC100 / BANC150 with BANC150 > 0.
- **Rules.** A college whose standard bachelor’s length exceeds four years is unavailable on this dimension; one with only some longer programs is scored and flagged, and every result is repeated without flagged colleges.

## 4. The selection stages (item 11)

Outcome: closure, merger or deletion in the outcome window under the primary coding, with the three components also reported separately. Every comparison is made on matched coverage, the institutions with every compared dimension available, and the loss of coverage is reported with how the covered institutions differ from the rest in sector, size, wealth and closure rate. The baseline in every comparison is a logistic model of size and sector alone, fitted on the same institutions.

- **Stage 1 (each gap alone; earlier scoring year).** For each dimension and version (the price gap; the outcome gap with retention alone, with nursing licensure alone where available, and with licensure at a minimum of 30 test-takers against 50 and 100; the four-year gap), the gain in area under the ROC curve of size, sector and that dimension over size and sector alone, with a paired bootstrap interval (2,000 resamples, fixed seed). Also reported: which gap was largest before each closure (descriptive).
- **Which advance to stage 2 (Decision B, approved).** Rank the dimensions by their stage-1 gain and carry forward the top three with a positive gain (fewer if fewer are positive); the outcome-gap version with the largest gain is the one carried.
- **Stage 2 (combinations; earlier scoring year).** The carried dimensions together, both combined by the Spine’s maximum (Definition 6.3.4b) and as separate features.
- **Stage 3 (confirmation; later scoring year).** Your pre-registered prediction (November 17): each channel has several parts, and measuring them together is more accurate than any one part. Three models are compared on held-out institutions: (1) the best single gap from stage 1, (2) all carried gaps combined by the maximum, (3) all carried gaps as separate features, each with size and sector. Supported if both (2) and (3) beat (1), each with a paired 95% interval for the difference above zero; “in the predicted direction, not separated from zero” if both point estimates favor them but an interval includes zero; not supported otherwise. (2) against (1) also tests the Spine’s claim that the combined divergence outperforms its parts. The same comparison is repeated for the meta channel once its parts exist (exit alone, voice alone, both).
- **What stage 3 decides.** Only which dimensions enter v4. Performance measured here is selection-stage performance, used to choose, and is never presented as an estimate of v4’s accuracy; v4 is evaluated in its own pre-registered tests.

## 5. The nursing pilot

Nursing licensure is the one outside licensure measure kept (November 20). Before collecting all fifty state boards, a pilot in states whose boards publish long, program-level, first-time pass-rate series:

- **Pilot states.** Oklahoma (one table of first-time NCLEX-RN pass rates by program for 2015 to 2025), Indiana (separate bachelor’s, associate and practical-nursing tables for 2015 to 2025, with test-taker counts), Texas (annual first-time pass rates by program, with counts, in the Board of Nursing’s reports) and Florida (program-specific annual reports, mandated by the legislature since 2017).
- **Matching.** Each program is matched to its IPEDS institution by name and city in a crosswalk committed before any pilot result is computed; unmatched programs are listed.
- **When the pilot counts as a success (Decision C, approved).** The pilot states will hold too few closures among development colleges to judge closure prediction on their own, so the criterion is: collect all fifty states if (a) at least 80% of nursing programs at four-year development institutions in the pilot states can be matched, (b) the frozen nursing calibration predicts reported completion out of sample at least as well as the retention calibration does in the same institutions, and (c) the nursing outcome gap is not associated with closure in the opposite direction from the one predicted (point estimate). Otherwise the outcome gap uses retention alone.

## 6. Records

Every step writes its result to data/v4 channels/ and is committed before the next begins: the field check; this protocol; the calibrations (step 1, with their diagnostics); the gap values for every institution and year (with every flag, the source of each input and the available set); and the stage results. Errors found along the way are recorded as amendments, never edited out.

## 7. Amendments

Amendments are dated, recorded here and never edited out of the sections above; the record of what was run before each amendment stays in the repository's history.

### Amendment 1 (November 20, 2026): income-band fields for private colleges

Section 3.1 names the income-band fields GIS4N12 to GIS4N52 over GIS4N2. Those are the public-college fields, which count in-state aid recipients only; private nonprofit and for-profit colleges report the same five bands for all aid recipients under GRN4N12 to GRN4N52 over GRN4N2 (confirmed in the SFA dictionaries by the field check). The first step 1 run (calibrations.json committed November 20, before this amendment) read only the public fields, so every income-band row was a public college and every private college fell to the Pell-share fit. Corrected: the bands are read from GIS4 for public colleges and from GRN4 for private nonprofit and for-profit colleges, the field used is recorded for every college-year (`income band field`), and the income-band fit, with control as a predictor, is refitted on both. The two families differ in whom they count (in-state recipients at public colleges, all recipients at private ones); control absorbs the difference in level, and the field is a stratification variable in every price-gap analysis. Step 1 is rerun and its calibrations frozen again. No closure outcome had been read.

### Amendment 2 (November 20, 2026): the selection-stage details left open

Fixed before any closure outcome is read:

- **Size in the baseline.** Size is the natural log of 12-month full-time-equivalent enrollment (EFIA: FTEUG + FTEGD + FTEDPP) for the scoring year's fiscal year, the value step 1 already reads; sector is the institution's control (public, private nonprofit, for-profit; every development institution is four-year). The baseline is a logistic model of these alone, refitted on the same institutions as each comparison.
- **Out-of-sample scoring.** Every AUC in stages 1 and 2 is computed on out-of-fold predictions: stratified 10-fold cross-validation with a fixed seed (20261120), so that each institution is scored by a model fitted without it. The paired bootstrap of section 4 (2,000 resamples, fixed seed) resamples institutions with those predictions held fixed.
- **The outcome gap in stage 1.** As the Spine states for the v4 model, overclaiming (D_over) and underclaiming (D_under) enter as two separate features; the direction-free outcome gap is reported beside them for information and is not a selection candidate.

### Open item (November 20, 2026): the program-length check of the four-year gap

Section 3.3 marks a college whose standard bachelor's length exceeds four years unavailable on the four-year gap, and flags one with only some longer programs. No IPEDS field states a college's standard program length, so the check is not yet built: every college with a GR200 record is scored, and every output says the check is not built. A source for program length is to be found and fixed by amendment before stage 1 is run; if none is found, stage 1 reports the four-year gap with this limitation stated.

### Amendment 3 (November 20, 2026): the program-length check, resolved

The open item above is resolved without a program-length field. The IPEDS cooperative-education indicator was considered and not used: it records whether a college offers cooperative education at all, not whether its standard bachelor's takes longer than four years, so it would flag many colleges whose students finish in four years. Decided instead: every college with a GR200 record is scored on the four-year gap, and every four-year-gap result states that the check of Definition 15.2.13 is not made. The direction of the resulting error is known: a college whose standard bachelor's is longer than four years shows a large four-year gap whatever its condition, and since such colleges are few and mostly large and stable, the error can only weaken the four-year gap's association with closure, never strengthen it; a four-year gap that is carried despite it is carried conservatively. One targeted check is added: if the four-year gap is carried from stage 1, the 30 development institutions with the largest four-year gaps are listed (by name and gap only, without outcomes) and looked up in their catalogs; any whose standard bachelor's length exceeds four years is marked unavailable on this dimension by name, and stages 1 and 2 are repeated without them, with both results reported.

### Amendment 4 (November 20, 2026): what stage 3 confirms, and when a gap counts as confirmed

Fixed after the stage 1 and 2 results and an exploratory repeat on the 2014 frame (data/v4 channels/"exploratory public and nonprofit.json"), and before any 2019 outcome is read.

- **What stage 3 confirms.** (a) As committed: the price gap, the one dimension carried by Decision B, on the whole 2019 frame. (b) Added, and labelled as chosen from the exploratory result: the four-year gap, on public and private nonprofit institutions only. In the whole 2014 frame, size and sector reached an AUC of 0.91 to 0.93, driven by for-profit campus closures, and no gap added value; among public and private nonprofit institutions size and sector reached about 0.77, and the four-year gap added +0.019 (95% interval 0.000 to 0.038). The 2019 frame has not been looked at, so stage 3 remains a clean test of both.
- **When a gap counts as confirmed.** A gap enters v4 (for the group in which it is confirmed) only if its gain in out-of-sample AUC over size and sector on the 2019 frame has a paired 95% bootstrap interval above zero; otherwise it does not enter. The several-parts prediction is not testable on these gaps, since no two gaps are confirmed together for the same group; it remains open for the meta channel.
- **The program-length check (Amendment 3) applied.** Because the four-year gap is now carried, the institutions with the largest four-year gaps were listed. In the public and nonprofit groups, 89 institutions in the 2014 frame and 71 in the 2019 frame report no bachelor's completer within four years (r_on = 0, the largest possible gap); most are very small (rabbinical colleges, Bible colleges, tribal colleges, small Puerto Rican institutions), where a zero can reflect a longer standard program, a very small cohort or a reporting convention. Three whose standard bachelor's is known to exceed four years are marked unavailable on the four-year gap by name: Northeastern University (five-year cooperative-education degrees), the Southern California Institute of Architecture and the Boston Architectural College (five-year Bachelor of Architecture). Because the remaining zeros cannot each be verified, stage 3 also reports the four-year result without every institution with r_on = 0, as a sensitivity analysis; the verdict rests on the main analysis, and both are reported together.

### Amendment 5 (November 20, 2026): the test of prediction P1

P1 (Spine §15.2.1, Definition 15.2.8) is tested on both development frames, separately, with the choices the Spine leaves open fixed here before the test is run. Baseline t0: the first fiscal year from t − 5 onward at which the price gap is available, so the widening runs over the same five-year window for each frame (2009 to 2014 for the 2014 frame; 2014 to 2019 for the 2019 frame), and only where the price gap is available at both years under the same finance form and the same need fit. Widened: above the 75th percentile of the widening among the frame's colleges of the same control. Price not cut: the published price at the scoring year, computed with the in-state weight of t0, at least its value at t0, in nominal dollars, with an inflation-adjusted version (CPI-U annual averages) as a sensitivity check. Primary group: private nonprofit colleges, standing in for "tuition-dependent private colleges" because the committed inputs hold no revenue breakdown; all colleges and each control are also reported, and a tuition-dependence measure (net tuition as a share of total revenue) is a later sensitivity step. Outcome: closure, merger or deletion after the scoring year under the primary coding of protocol v2. Reported: closure rates of flagged and other colleges, the ratio of the rates with a 95% interval, and Fisher's exact test (one-sided, flagged more often, as P1 predicts; two-sided also given). The price gap's limitation applies: the widening is relative to comparable practice.

### Amendment 6 (November 20, 2026): the soundness dimension (the v4 meta channel)

The soundness dimension of Spine Definitions 15.2.15 and 15.2.16 is built and tested on the same development frames, with these choices fixed before any of its values is computed. Field names were confirmed in every year 2012 to 2023 by the soundness field check (data/v4 channels/"soundness field check.json", workflow v4_soundness_field_check.yml).

- **Exit (meta reading).** FT(t): full-time staff, all occupations, fall t (Human Resources S{t}_OC, FTPT = 2, OCCUPCAT = 100, HRTOTLT). NH(t): all full-time new hires reported for t (S{t}_NH, SNHCAT = 10000, HRTOTLT). The staffing files begin in fall 2012, so x_exit exists from 2013. IPEDS counts new hires only between July 1 and October 31, so x_exit is a residual under a headcount model, as the Spine states, and is labelled so.
- **Operating coverage (operational reading).** B_cov = total revenues / total expenses, with the fields Tell already uses: F1D01 / F1D02 (public), F2B01 / F2B02 (private nonprofit), F3D09 / F3B02 (for-profit); fiscal year t reads the finance file F{t−1}{t}. Private revenue includes investment return, so coverage moves with markets at endowed colleges; stated as a limitation wherever it is reported.
- **Wealth bands for the exit norm.** Private nonprofit colleges are banded by end-of-year endowment per FTE (F2H02) and public colleges by state appropriations per FTE (F1B11), as the Spine provides, with FTE from the 12-month enrollment step 1 reads. Terciles within each control, fixed on the norm years. For-profit colleges report no endowment and are compared with their sector's pooled norm, flagged.
- **Norm years.** x_norm (median exit rate by control and wealth band, with the Spine's cell rules, m_min = 20) and x_ref (95th percentile of excess exit by control, with n_pos = 20; 90th and 97.5th as sensitivity alternatives) are fitted on the staffing years 2013 and 2014 only, over every institution in either development frame, and frozen; neither frame takes values from after its scoring year.
- **Readings at the scoring year t** (2014 and 2019): s_p = min(1, B_cov(t)), s_ins = 1 − min(1, x_ex(t) / x_ref), D_op^sound = g_JS(1 − s_p), D_om^sound = g_JS(1 − s_ins), D_pm^sound = JS(s_p, s_ins), with the sign of s_p − s_ins recorded.
- **Selection.** The stages of section 4 with the settings of Amendment 2 (size and sector baseline, out-of-fold AUC, paired bootstrap): stage 1 on the 2014 frame, coverage alone (D_op^sound) and exit alone (D_om^sound); Decision B carries those with a positive gain; stage 2 on the 2014 frame, the carried readings combined by the maximum and as separate features (with D_pm^sound among the separate features when both are carried). Stage 3 on the 2019 frame, committed separately after stages 1 and 2: the several-parts prediction is tested here as committed in section 4 (best single reading against the maximum and against the separate features, both intervals above zero for "supported"), and a reading enters v4 only if its gain over size and sector has an interval above zero (Amendment 4).

### Amendment 7 (November 20, 2026): two comparability segments for exit

The first soundness values run (committed November 20, before this amendment; no closure outcome read) showed the median estimated exit rate roughly doubling between staffing years 2015 and 2016 in every sector (private nonprofit about 6% to 11%, public about 4.5% to 8%). The cause is a change in the IPEDS definition of new hires: through 2015 the Human Resources survey counted full-time permanent staff hired between July 1 and October 31; from 2016 it counts those hired over a full twelve months (November 1 to October 31), and from 2018 it states that new hires are reported whether or not they are still on the payroll on November 1. As Spine §15.2.1 requires for a definition change, this starts a new comparability segment. Decided: segment A (staffing years 2013 to 2015) and segment B (2016 on) have separate norms, cells and reference levels, fixed by the same rules: segment A on staffing years 2013 and 2014 (used for the 2014 frame, unchanged), segment B on staffing years 2016 and 2017 (used for the 2019 frame; both before the scoring year). Every output records the segment used. Segment B is the closer measure of annual departures, since it counts a full year of hires.

### Amendment 8 (November 20, 2026): the later dimensions of item 12 (endowment draw, value, law schools)

Fixed before any value of these dimensions is computed and before any outcome is read for them. The 2014 and 2019 frames have already been used in the stages above, so this is a test of new dimensions on frames whose outcomes are known in aggregate; no result for these dimensions has been seen. Bond disclosures against actuals (EMMA) are dropped: the bulk data cost $22,500 a year (decision of November 20).

- **Field checks first.** Before any value is computed, a field check (data dictionaries only; no data, no outcome) confirms, for every year 2009 to 2023, the IPEDS finance fields for the endowment (beginning and end values, and the spending distribution for current use with its companion lines, new gifts, net investment return and other changes, wherever reported) and, for every College Scorecard file in the repository's release asset scorecard-raw-data-2026-11-02, the repayment, cohort default and earnings variables, with the definition change in the repayment measures recorded as a comparability break, as Spine §15.2.1 requires (two segments, as for exit in Amendment 7). The check also records whether the release asset holds the field-of-study files that item 13 needs. Every field name used below is the one the check confirms; if a needed field is absent for a scoring year, that year falls to the fallback stated for it, and the output says so.

- **Endowment draw (private nonprofit colleges).** r_draw(t) = spending distribution for current use / endowment value at the beginning of fiscal year t, with fiscal year t read from the finance file F{t−1}{t}, as in Amendment 6. Where the spending distribution is not reported for the scoring year, the draw is backed out as (beginning value + new gifts + net investment return − end value) / beginning value, which counts other changes (transfers, write-downs) as spending, and every such value is labelled backed out. The comparison is a conventional spending band of 4 to 5% of the beginning value, labelled an approximation in every output: it stands in for each college's own stated spending rule, which IPEDS does not record. Readings: overdraw D_draw = max(0, r_draw − 0.05), the primary feature; underdraw max(0, 0.04 − r_draw), reported for information only. Colleges with a beginning value of zero or none reported are not scored on this dimension and are reported as their own group with their closure rate (the D2 rule). Public colleges are not scored: their endowment fields are empty in most years (November 14 early-finance check).

- **The closure floor for the endowment draw.** Closures concentrate among small private nonprofits with little or no endowment, so before stage 1 a count step reports, for each frame, only the number of private nonprofit colleges scored on the draw and the number of them that closed (no draw value is joined to any outcome). If both frames hold at least 40 closures among scored colleges (the event floor of protocol v2), the draw is tested by the stages below. If either frame holds fewer, it is not tested: it is reported descriptively instead (closure rates of colleges above the band, inside it and below it, in each frame, with 95% intervals), stated as too few events to test.

- **Value dimension (all controls).** Primary reading: College Scorecard repayment rate, D_value = 1 − repayment rate, from the latest Scorecard file whose academic year ends no later than the scoring year (for scoring year 2014, the 2013–14 file or earlier; for 2019, the 2018–19 file or earlier), so no value is read from after the scoring year. The files in the release asset were recompiled in 2026; the cohort measures they hold refer to cohorts before the file's year, and this is stated as a limitation. Secondary readings, reported for information and not selection candidates: the three-year cohort default rate, and median earnings ten years after entry against the median of the same control. Repayment rates are reported at the level of the federal aid identifier (OPEID6), which chain campuses share. Decided: in the primary analysis each identifier counts once, with every campus sharing it weighted 1/k (k campuses in the frame) and the bootstrap resampling identifiers rather than campuses; the campus-level, unweighted result is reported beside it as a check. Colleges with no Scorecard value are reported as their own group with their closure rate.

- **Selection (endowment draw if it meets the floor; value dimension).** As in Amendment 6, with the settings of Amendment 2: stage 1 on the 2014 frame, each primary reading alone against size and sector (the endowment draw among private nonprofit colleges only, with size alone as the baseline there); Decision B carries those with a positive gain; stage 2 on the 2014 frame, the carried readings combined by the maximum and as separate features, where two are carried for the same group. Stage 3 on the 2019 frame, committed separately after stages 1 and 2: a reading enters v4 only if its gain over the baseline has a paired 95% interval above zero (Amendment 4), and the several-parts prediction is tested as committed in section 4 wherever two readings are confirmed for the same group.

- **Law schools (descriptive record, not a test).** About six ABA-approved law schools have closed since 2017 (Whittier, Charlotte, Arizona Summit, Valparaiso, Savannah, Florida Coastal), far below any event floor, so ABA Standard 509 outcomes are not tested for closure prediction. Source: the ABA's national 509 compilation spreadsheets, one per year, downloaded once by hand if the ABA's site refuses GitHub Actions, and kept in data/v4 channels/law schools/ with the download date. Record: for each closed school, its first-time bar passage rate, full-time bar-passage-required employment ten months after graduation, first-year attrition and first-year enrollment for each of the five reporting years before its closure (or the years available), each as a percentile among all ABA schools that year. Expectation, written before any value is looked up: each closed school sat in the bottom decile on first-time bar passage or on employment in at least two of the three years before it closed. The list of closed schools is fixed from the ABA's own records before any 509 value is read, and any school added to or removed from the list is named with the reason.

- **Records.** Every step writes its result to data/v4 channels/ (the law-school record to data/v4 channels/law schools/) and is committed before the next begins: the field checks, the count step, the values, and each stage's results.

### Amendment 9 (November 20, 2026): adjunct absorption (item 17), exploratory

Fixed before any of these measures is computed and before any outcome is joined to them. IPEDS counts staff by category each fall and does not follow individuals, and it counts new hires for full-time staff only, so adjunct retention, moves from part-time to the tenure track, internal against external hiring and publication rates are not measured here; they are recorded as needing hand-collected data (decision of November 20). The 2014 frame's outcomes are known in aggregate, so this is exploratory, and the prediction is tested blind with the HD2026 retest.

- **Files and categories.** The employees-by-position file (EAP{y}), the full-time new-hire file (S{y}_NH), fall 2012 to the latest served. Every category is found by its label in that year's data dictionary (occupation: instructional staff, total; faculty status: all, with faculty status total, tenured, on tenure track, not on tenure track total, annual contract, less-than-annual contract, without faculty status), never by an assumed code; a category whose label does not match exactly one code in a year is recorded as unresolved, and the measures that need it are not computed for that year. Within a resolved category an institution with no row has none of that staff.
- **Measures, by institution and fall year.** (1) Part-time pool share: part-time instructional headcount / all instructional headcount. (2) Contract mix: full-time instructional staff with faculty status on annual or less-than-annual contracts / all full-time instructional staff with faculty status. (3) Full-time instructional turnover, as the exit residual of Amendment 6 restricted to instructional staff and computed separately for the tenure line (tenured plus tenure track) and off the track (not on tenure track plus without faculty status): max(0, NH(t) − (FT(t) − FT(t−1))) / FT(t−1), with the two comparability segments of Amendment 7 (through fall 2015; fall 2016 on). (4) Pool depletion: (PT(t) − PT(t−1)) / PT(t−1), part-time instructional headcount.
- **The crossing-curves prediction at equal wealth.** Frame: the 2014 development frame, readings at fall 2014 (the staffing year the soundness dimension uses for that frame). Equal wealth: the wealth terciles frozen for segment A of the exit norm (endowment per FTE for private nonprofits, state appropriations per FTE for publics); for-profit colleges form one pooled cell, flagged; public and private nonprofit colleges with no wealth value (no endowment or appropriations reported) form their own cell within their control, so they are compared only with each other. Heavy absorbers: colleges in the top quarter of part-time pool share within their control and wealth tercile; all others are the comparison. Prediction, written before any curve is computed: heavy absorbers close less often in the first years (cumulative closure rate lower through 2017) and more often by the end of the window (higher by the latest header file), so the curves cross. Reported: cumulative closure rates by year from 2015 for both groups, overall and by control, the first year the heavy-absorber curve is above the comparison's, and the difference at 2017 and at the latest year with a 95% bootstrap interval (2,000 resamples, fixed seed). Outcome: closure, merger or deletion after 2014 under the primary coding of protocol v2, dated by the header file in which its first standing ground appears (or the first year after the institution's last appearance for a disappearance). Supported if the 2017 difference is below zero and the latest-year difference is above zero, each with its interval excluding zero; "in the predicted direction" if both point estimates are, with an interval including zero; not supported otherwise.
- **Descriptive record.** Medians of all four measures by control and fall year, and for heavy absorbers against the comparison, are reported beside the test; none of them is tested against closure here.
